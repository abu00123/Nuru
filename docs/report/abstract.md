# ABSTRACT

Refugees considering voluntary return need current, reliable information about conditions in the places they may return to, yet such information is often scattered, undated, and difficult to judge. This study addressed that problem for Burundian refugees in Mahama Refugee Settlement, Rwanda, with the aim of designing, developing, and evaluating a system that helps users find and assess return-related information. Requirements were gathered through an online questionnaire completed by 25 ALU students who live or have lived in Mahama. The resulting system, Nuru, was built iteratively with React, Django REST Framework, and PostgreSQL, together with a mobile prototype. It organises information by category and destination, shows each entry's source and dates, and requires a source review tied to the exact version of an entry before publication. Participants most needed information on safety and changing local conditions, and most often reported outdated and conflicting information as difficulties. All 30 automated tests passed, audited pages reached Lighthouse accessibility scores of 100, and five usability participants completed every task, 72% of attempts without help, and rated the system positively. However, no participant understood that a last-checked date does not confirm current conditions, a misunderstanding traced to "Verified" labels in the interface. The study concludes that version-checked, source-attributed content is a sound basis for responsible return information, but that interface wording must state precisely what checking establishes. It recommends relabelling, delivery through messaging channels, and evaluation with current Mahama residents.

**Keywords:** voluntary return, refugee information, source credibility, content verification, Burundi, Mahama
